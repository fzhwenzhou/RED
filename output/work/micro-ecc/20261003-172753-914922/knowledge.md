
micro-ecc / secp256r1.mmod Gate 3 lesson (2026-10-03): the compressed column-sum C model of the NIST P-256 reduction executed only ~514 host instructions, while the kernel runs ~8,168 per call. Gate 3 (red/cost.py) then (a) flagged invocations as UNVERIFIED (implied = kernel_ir/model_ir ~16) and (b) applied the _WORK_MISMATCH clamp: it credited only 514*8.5 = 4,374 cycles per invocation out of 69,428 per call, which left ~87% of the 48.5%-share kernel in software. Whole-app was 1.88x even though mmod looked like 10.9x on its own line. Fix in v1.1: the model now follows the kernel's own FIPS 186-4 D.2.3 schedule (ten 8-word add/sub passes s1,s1,s2,s2,s3,s4,-d1..-d4 plus eight constant-time masked correction steps; t in [-4,6], <=5 adds or <=7 subs needed). Outputs are bit-identical to the old model and the hardware is honestly charged for the adder passes. invocations stays at 2 (data says 1; write_spec's 40-IR-per-word static bound rejects 1). For a declared n, the model IR must be >= kernel_ir/(4n) to avoid the credit clamp and >= kernel_ir/(2n) to avoid the UNVERIFIED flag.
