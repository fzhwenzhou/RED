Let q=2^16, M=2^256, p=2^256-2^224+2^192+2^96-1 and K=M-p=2^224-2^192-2^96+1. Let T=2^(256+16*k), Q=K*2^(16*k), and X0=LE512(C_entry). Each pass is nonnegative and no larger than its input, because it removes an existing digit at T and replaces it with the smaller positive contribution Q. It preserves residue modulo p. There is no hidden modular truncation of a value above 512 bits.
Software must call fold with k=15,14,...,0, once for each of the SIXTEEN 16-bit digits above bit 255. The order is fixed, independent of values. This produces canonical C in [0,p). Proof: at step k, X<T*q. Since Q<T/q^2, the first pass is below T+T/q. If its digit at T is zero, the second leaves it unchanged. Otherwise that digit is one and the low part is below T/q, so the second pass is below T/q+Q<T. The subtraction only decreases X. Descending k therefore leaves X<2^256, and p>2^255 means the final single subtraction normalizes it. Two passes are necessary to handle carry back into the just-eliminated position; both always execute.
